The app

In short

This policy covers the app “Days of the Week and Months” (called “Calendar” on the device). The app collects no data about your child. There is no account, no server, no advertising and no analytics. The app works without the internet; everything that happens while your child learns stays on this device.

What is stored on the device

In its own storage area the app keeps only what it needs to carry on: which rounds are done and with how many stars, where the companion stands on the three learning paths, how many free rounds have been played, whether the full version has been bought, and the settings (companion, sound, language, whether the introduction has been shown, counters for the question about a rating).

The app never asks for your child’s name, age, photo or voice. We have no access to any of this data.

Your child’s birthday

If parents enter their child’s birthday, the app stores only the day and month on this device; none of it leaves the device, and the entry can be deleted under “For grown-ups”.

The field is optional and sits in the grown-ups’ area behind the grown-up question; the child is never asked. No name and no year of birth are stored. The app uses the entry only to show the birthday on the calendar in the Today view and to say how many more sleeps there are until then.

The device’s date

The Today view shows the day of the week, the month and the season from the device’s date. The app reads it afresh each time and does not store it. It does not ask for your location or time zone.

Voice and sounds

All spoken prompts, words of praise and sounds are audio files inside the app; they are only played, never downloaded. The app does not use the device’s text-to-speech, records nothing and does not access the microphone.

Backups on Android devices

The app is excluded from cloud backup and from transfer to a new device. After reinstalling, it really starts from scratch; you get your purchase back with “Restore purchase”.

Purchase and permissions on Google Play

The app is free to download; the first twelve rounds are free, and a one-time purchase unlocks all rounds for good, with no subscription. The purchase runs entirely through Google Play. Google processes payment and purchase data under its own responsibility; we never learn a name or email address. The app keeps only a note on the device that the purchase was made. It is restored through the Google account on the device; the app needs no account of its own.

The installed app has three permissions, all of them from the built-in Google Play purchase library: the Google Play billing permission, internet access and checking the network state. The app itself does not connect to any server – we do not run one. It does not ask for the camera, microphone, contacts, photos or location.

Purchase and permissions on the Amazon Appstore

The app is free to download; the first twelve rounds are free, and a one-time purchase unlocks all rounds for good, with no subscription. The Amazon Appstore app on the device handles the purchase. Amazon processes payment and purchase data under its own responsibility; we never learn a name or email address. The app keeps only a note on the device that the purchase was made. It is restored through the Amazon account on the device; the app needs no account of its own.

The installed app has two permissions, both from the built-in purchase libraries: internet access and checking the network state. The app itself does not connect to any server – we do not run one. It does not ask for the camera, microphone, contacts, photos or location.

Links in the grown-ups’ area

“Send a message” opens your email app; only the subject, the app and system version and the store are filled in. “Rate the app” shows the store’s rating window or opens the app’s store page, and “From the same maker” opens store pages of our other apps. The app only hands over a link, reads nothing and sends nothing itself. Each of these steps is behind the grown-up question.

The app store

If you download the app from an app store, the store operator processes its own data about the download and installation. The store operator is responsible for that. The store passes on to us only order data needed for accounting and tax (order number, country and, in some countries, region and postal code) – never a name or an email address.

Children

The app is made for children aged 5 to 8 and collects no personal information from children. It has no account, no chat and no way of sending us anything by itself, and no third party collects data through it. If a child emails us anyway, we reply once, use the address for nothing else, do not share it and delete the message after replying. Parents can ask us at any time to find and delete such a message, using the contact details below.

Email from adults

If you write to us, we use your address and message only to reply and delete the message once your request is settled and no retention duty applies. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR if it is about a purchase, otherwise Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR – our legitimate interest in answering you. Our mailbox is hosted by Hetzner Online GmbH in Germany under a data processing agreement.

To spot wishes and bugs, we also analyse the text of messages with an AI service: Claude by Anthropic (USA). We remove email addresses and phone numbers first, and the sender address is not passed on. The transfer to the USA is based on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR).

Deleting data

You delete all of the app’s data by uninstalling it or by clearing its data in the device settings. You delete the birthday on its own under “For grown-ups”.

This website

Hosting (GitHub Pages)

This website is hosted by GitHub, Inc., 88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. St., San Francisco, CA 94107, USA, as a “GitHub Pages” site. When you open a page, GitHub processes technically necessary data in server logs, in particular your device’s IP address. The purpose is to deliver the page and to keep it running securely and reliably. We ourselves have no access to these logs.

The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR – our legitimate interest in operating the website securely and reliably. Transfer to the USA: GitHub is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and additionally offers Standard Contractual Clauses as a safeguard. GitHub decides how long the logs are kept; for details, see GitHub’s privacy statement: https://docs.github.com/privacy

No cookies, no external resources

This website sets no cookies and embeds no external fonts, scripts or trackers – all resources are served from the same server. That is why there is no cookie banner here: there is nothing to consent to.

Links to app stores

The store badges on this website are stored on our own server. A link to Google Play or the Amazon Appstore is only followed when you click it; before that, no data goes to Google or Amazon. Once you follow a link, the respective provider processes data as its own controller, and its privacy policy applies.

Your rights and complaints

Under the GDPR and the UK GDPR, you have the right to access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18) and data portability (Art. 20), and the right to object (Art. 21).

Right to object under Art. 21 GDPR: Where we process data on the basis of our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) – the server logs of this website and the handling of your emails – you can object at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation. We will then stop processing the data unless we can demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms.

In the app itself these rights have nothing to act on, because we hold no data about you or your child. They do apply to emails you send us. For the server logs, GitHub is the right addressee, as we have no access to them; for purchases, payment data and purchase history, Google or Amazon.

If you have a complaint, please contact us first at kontakt@remindery.de. We confirm receipt within 30 days and tell you what we are doing about it. You can also complain to a data protection authority: in the UK to the Information Commissioner’s Office (https://ico.org.uk), in the EU to the authority in the country where you live.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app or the operation of this website requires different processing, we will update this policy, publish it in the app and on this page, and change the date at the top. The version published here applies.
